Halting and Reducing Proliferation of Ocean-based Narcotics (HARPOON) Act

Section 1: Existing Gaps in Coast Guard Jurisdiction

In July 2025, the Colombian Navy intercepted the first unmanned, remotely-guided narco-submarine in the Caribbean. Existing legal frameworks that position the US Coast Guard as the predominant jurisdictional authority when it comes to the policing of maritime drug trade are facing severe gaps when it comes to addressing these new technologies employed by traffickers. Current judicial infrastructure was designed to prosecute human perpetrators. An autonomous vessel means that there is no one to arrest, charge, or claim vessel nationality from. This can be exploited to evade criminal liability, increasing the difficulty of addressing maritime drug trade.

In addition to autonomous submarines, maritime drug trafficking operations have exploded in innovations through the advent of other developments like GPS-guided drug buoys, aerial drone surveillance, and Starlink-equipped trafficking vessels, just to name a few. The problem is not just autonomous submarines—the different technologies employed by drug trafficking operations have diversified and advanced. This leaves contemporary strategies insufficient in properly regulating drug trade.

Section 2: Existing Solutions

This bill will not propose the development of novel technologies for the US Coast Guard. It is not an efficient strategy to address each and every new drug trafficking innovation with a separate innovation for the Coast Guard. Instead, this bill will arrange to make use of existing infrastructure to ensure that the Coast Guard is better equipped to handle evolving threats.

Section 3: Applications of Joint All-Domain Command and Control (JADC2) in Regulating Drug Trade

Joint All-Domain Command and Control (JADC2) is a concept being actively developed by the United States Department of War. It is an effort to link sensors from all branches of the Armed Forces to provide warfighters with a cohesive picture of the battlespace. By providing soldiers with information from air, land, sea, space, and cyber domains, JADC2 aims to streamline decision making and make warfighting more efficient in general.

Recent(?) counter-narcotics efforts in the Caribbean throughout the fall and winter of 2025 leading up to the United States Invasion of Venezuela demonstrated capabilities within the Air Force, Navy, and Marine Corps to employ “blended operations” to effectively counter activities of narco-terrorist group Tren de Aragua. Air Force assets like the MQ-9B Reaper and the F-35A Lightning II collaborated with Navy guided missile destroyers like the USS Jason Dunham and USS Stockdale to deliver thousands of kinetic strikes on maritime vessels ferrying narcotics.

The idea is that JADC2 operation structures are brought to America’s war on drugs, just as they were with the buildup in the Caribbean in late 2025. Air Force, Navy, Marine Corps, and Space Force assets will all be relegated in efficient ways to combating drug trafficking operations. From surveillance, armed reconnaissance, intelligence gathering, and digital tracking, different branches of the Armed Forces will operate in a networked domain to fill the capability gaps of the Coast Guard.

In a hypothetical scenario, Coast Guard above-surface patrols can be supplemented by autonomous underwater surveillance sentries belonging to the Navy, while flights of Air Force drones overhead provide armed reconnaissance for every interception.

Section 4: Technical Details

The first phases of this shift towards all-domain operations will comprise the relegation of military assets not currently tasked with intensive tasks in active conflicts or deterrence missions. Counternarcotics JADC2 operations will NOT detract from active assignments in contested environments like the Indo-Pacific, the Middle East, and NATO support missions.

The following is an envisioning of how certain military assets will be shifted to counternarcotics operations as pure examples:

Preliminary Air Force assets will be drawn from Air National Guard reserves. Elements like the 147th Attack Wing (Texas Air National Guard), and the 163rd Attack Wing (California Air National Guard) are excellent examples for their geographic proximity to areas commonly traversed by maritime drug trafficking operations. These elements are also currently tasked with defending homeland airspace and domestic operations, suggesting that they may be relegated to new missions without leaving a huge gap in responsibility that must be filled by someone else.

Preliminary Naval assets will be drawn from fleets of autonomous vessels as well as destroyers that are already assigned to the United States Southern Command area of responsibility. Arleigh Burke-class destroyers like the USS Gridley and the USS Sampson already regularly cycle through the Caribbean and Latin America to conduct counternarcotics operations, and thus are prime assets to supplement Coast Guard operations.

Section 5: Financial Details

Efforts to upscale JADC2 counternarcotics operations will require financial support that must come in the form of expansions to the Department of War’s annual budget. The following are several examples of certain areas that will require increased financial support and attention.

  1. Upscaling of AI and battlespace management infrastructure used to support networking
    1. $400-$500 million will be added to the Air Force Advanced Battle Management System
    2. $250-$300 million will be added to expand the Navy’s Project Overmatch network architecture
  2. Modernized hardware investments
    1. $150-$200 million will be added to the budgets of Marine Corps littoral regiments and Coast Guard cutters to adopt cloud nodes for connectivity
    2. $100 million will be allocated to upgrade Coast Guard C-130J aircraft with Link-16 datalink capabilities to better communicate with other assets
    3. $600-$800 million will be allocated to the Space Force for the accelerated development of Low Earth Orbit tracking systems
  3. Expansions to existing program offices
    1. $300 million will be allocated to the Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office to build and sustain automated data pipelines

While these figures may seem large amounts, it is important to consider that they are further augmentations of the Department of War’s already-gargantuan budgets for similar projects. In 2025 alone, $1.8 billion dollars were reserved for the development of AI and battlespace management software. As mentioned before, this bill hopes to augment—not deviate—from current shifts towards providing warfighters with better capabilities.

Section 6: Addressing Possible Concerns

A possible concern is that the relegation of the Air Force, Navy, and Marine Corps to joint domain operations in regulating drug trade may detract from their regular duties. Constant regulation of maritime drug trafficking may result in a “forever war” of sorts where a low-intensity problem constantly requires attention and resources from the Armed Forces.

This is a genuine concern that must be addressed, but it is worth considering whether this commitment may propose certain opportunities that could outweigh its costs. The Department of War every year expends $342 billion dollars on average in operation and maintenance bills used for military exercises and readiness training.

The idea is that applying JADC2 tactics to drug trafficking regulation operations can provide a “playground” of sorts where military branches are provided a low-intensity, permissible environment to employ new strategies and tactics in a productive manner.

Conclusion

The benefit of utilizing JADC2 infrastructure is that the Department of War has already invested considerable funds and effort into its development. Framing this reinterpretation of drug trade regulating as an extension of JADC2 is not a new, separate initiative, but in actuality an opportunity to further develop and accelerate a program that our Armed Forces have already devoted to.

Drug trafficking operations have evolved in unprecedented ways that make them no longer “low-intensity threats” that can be easily addressed. American war efforts have never featured one single branch relegated to fighting an entire enemy. From Spain in the 1890s to Iran in 2026, different branches of the military have always cooperated in a cohesive picture to support and protect America’s interests.

Now, as drug trafficking operations innovate in ways unprecedented, it is time to consider America’s war on drugs a true war—a true war that deserves the combined attention of America’s bravest soldiers.
